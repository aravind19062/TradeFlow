# Exchanges, brokers and market participants

## The exchange

A **stock exchange** runs the venue where buy and sell orders meet. It publishes rules, operates the
trading system and disseminates prices. In India the main equity exchanges are the NSE and the BSE.

## The broker

Retail investors cannot send orders to an exchange directly. A **stockbroker** that is a member of the
exchange accepts your orders, checks that you have the funds or shares, and routes the orders to the
exchange. The broker also completes **KYC** (know-your-customer) checks before opening your account.

## Clearing, settlement and the depository

After a trade, a **clearing corporation** makes sure both sides deliver: the buyer pays and the seller
delivers shares. Shares are held electronically in a **demat account** at a depository. Settlement in
Indian equities currently happens one business day after the trade (T+1). Settlement cycles are set by
regulators and can change.

## Who else is in the market

Long-term investors, short-term traders, mutual funds, insurance companies, foreign investors and
market makers all trade in the same market. Many of them have more information, faster technology and
more capital than an individual. That is one reason short-term trading is difficult.

## The regulator

Securities markets in India are regulated by **SEBI**. Regulation sets rules for exchanges, brokers and
listed companies. It does not protect you from losing money on a trade you chose to make.

> **Key idea:** every order you place passes through a broker, an exchange and a clearing system, and
> you are trading against other participants, many of them professionals.
