# What a stock is

A **share** (or stock) is a unit of ownership in a company. If a company has issued 1,00,00,000 shares and
you own 100, you own a tiny fraction of that business: its assets, its future profits and its risks.

## Why companies issue shares

Companies raise money by selling shares, first in an **initial public offering (IPO)** and sometimes
later. After listing, those shares change hands between investors on a **stock exchange**. The company does
not receive money when you buy a share from another investor on the exchange; you are trading with
another market participant.

## How a shareholder can gain or lose

* **Price change.** The share price moves as buyers and sellers change their view of the company's value.
  You gain if you sell above your purchase price and lose if you sell below it.
* **Dividends.** Some companies pay part of their profits to shareholders. Dividends are not guaranteed
  and can be cut.
* **Losing everything.** If a company fails, its shares can become worthless. Shareholders are paid after
  lenders and other creditors, so they are usually last in line.

## Equity is not a savings account

A share has no promised return and no fixed maturity. Prices can fall sharply and stay low for years.
Past performance tells you what happened, not what will happen.

> **Key idea:** owning a share means sharing in a business's uncertain outcomes, both good and bad.
