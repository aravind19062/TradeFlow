# How leverage and margin work

**Leverage** means controlling a position larger than your own money, using borrowed funds or derivatives.
**Margin** is the money you must deposit to hold a leveraged position.

## Leverage magnifies both directions

With ₹1,00,000 of your own money and 5× leverage you control ₹5,00,000 of exposure.

| Price move | Unleveraged result | 5× leveraged result |
|---|---|---|
| +4% | +₹4,000 (+4%) | +₹20,000 (+20%) |
| −4% | −₹4,000 (−4%) | −₹20,000 (−20%) |
| −20% | −₹20,000 (−20%) | −₹1,00,000 (**−100%**) |

A 20% fall, which is common for individual shares, wipes out the entire capital at 5× leverage.

## Where leverage appears

* **Intraday margin products** offered by brokers.
* **Futures and options (F&O):** small premiums or margins control large notional values.
* **Margin trading facilities:** borrowing from the broker to buy shares, with interest.

## Margin calls and forced closure

If losses reduce your margin below the required level, the broker will ask for more money (a **margin
call**) or may **close your positions without your consent**, often at the worst moment. Losses can
exceed the money you deposited.
