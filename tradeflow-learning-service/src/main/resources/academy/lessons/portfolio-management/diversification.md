# Diversification and allocation

## Concentration risk

If one holding is 40% of your portfolio, a 25% fall in that one share costs you 10% of everything.
Concentration magnifies the impact of any single company's bad news.

## Diversification

Spreading money across several companies, sectors and asset classes reduces the damage any single one
can do. It does **not** remove market risk: in a broad sell-off most shares fall together.

## Measuring concentration

* **Position weight:** the holding's market value divided by total portfolio value.
* **Herfindahl index (HHI):** the sum of squared weights within your holdings. 1.0 means one position;
  1/N means N equal positions. TradeFlow shows it, with the equivalent "effective number of holdings"
  (1 / HHI).

## Asset allocation

How you split money between shares, bonds, cash and other assets usually matters more to your overall
risk than which individual shares you pick. A suitable allocation depends on your goals, time horizon,
other savings and ability to bear losses. Those are personal questions. This course cannot answer them
for you, and passing it does not mean an allocation is suitable for you.

## Rebalancing

As prices move, weights drift. Rebalancing means periodically trimming what has grown too large and
adding to what has shrunk, to return to your intended allocation.
