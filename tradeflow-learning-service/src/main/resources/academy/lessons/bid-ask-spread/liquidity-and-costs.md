# Liquidity and the real cost of a trade

## Liquidity

A share is **liquid** when many buyers and sellers trade it continuously. Liquid shares usually have
narrow spreads and deep order books. **Illiquid** shares can have wide spreads, few shares at each price,
and prices that jump.

## Spreads widen when it matters most

Spreads typically widen around market open and close, during news, and in market stress. Exactly when
you most want to get out of a position, it can cost the most to do so.

## Every round trip has costs

A round trip (buy then sell) costs:

1. **The spread**, paid every time you trade at market.
2. **Charges:** brokerage, exchange transaction charges, securities transaction tax (STT), stamp duty,
   GST on fees and regulatory fees. Your broker's contract note itemises them.
3. **Slippage:** the difference between the price you expected and the price you got.
4. **Taxes on gains**, which depend on holding period and your circumstances.

Frequent trading multiplies these costs. A strategy that looks profitable before costs can be
unprofitable after them.

## In TradeFlow's demo

The demo models the spread but not brokerage, taxes or market impact. Real trading costs more than the
demo shows.

> **Key idea:** you pay the spread and charges on every trade, whether it wins or loses.
